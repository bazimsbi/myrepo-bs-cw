Executive summary

Corewell Health operates a functioning medallion architecture on a single AWS-hosted Snowflake account, orchestrated by Astronomer with Fivetran ingestion and dbt Core transformation. The platform is operationally sound and the tooling is not the problem — a position Corewell stated directly and which this review supports.

The constraint is structural. Core business entities are modeled independently by each domain, so there is no single canonical definition of Patient, Provider or Encounter; business metrics are redefined inside each BI tool; and there is no visible classification-to-masking chain protecting PHI. These are the conditions that produce competing versions of the truth, and they cannot be resolved by tooling changes.

This review identifies eleven gaps, three of them critical. The recommended response is three insertions rather than a rebuild: a conformed entity layer between Silver and Gold, a semantic layer above Gold, and a tag-driven governance overlay applied from Silver onward. Corewell's existing tool chain is retained in full.

The first eight weeks require no platform change — they consist of establishing the facts and proving the pattern on a single entity, which also produces the evidence the assessment workbook requires.

Section 1 · Current state

What Corewell runs today

A medallion architecture on a single AWS-hosted Snowflake account. Corewell reports no material problems with ingestion or processing tooling — the pipeline works. The issues sit in how data is modeled and governed once it lands.

Confirmed vs. inferred. The tool chain, single-account topology, batch-ELT approach and consumption tools come from this week's discovery call. The internal layer arrangement — Common, Workspace, and which tools read from where — is read off the architecture diagram and should be confirmed with Jay before this is presented as fact.

Section 2 · Gap analysis

Eleven gaps, ranked by exposure

Ordered by risk to Corewell, not by effort to fix. The top three block the unified-model outcome the client explicitly asked for, or carry regulatory exposure.

Critical

No conformed entity layer — every domain models core entities independently

There is no single canonical definition of Patient, Provider, Encounter, Facility or Payer. Each domain builds its own version inside Gold.

Why it matters — this is the single-source-of-truth problem Jay raised. Until core entities are defined once and reused, every downstream fix is cosmetic.

Critical

PHI/PII protection is not architecturally visibleconfirm

No classification → tagging → masking chain appears in the current design. Row access policies and column masking were named as a requirement, not as something already in place.

Why it matters — healthcare data under HIPAA. If PHI is readable in Silver, Workspace, or an Excel extract, controls at Gold are decorative.

Critical

One account holds every environment

Dev, Test and Prod are separated by database inside a single Snowflake account and region.

Why it matters — a misapplied grant or role change crosses environments. Blast radius, credit attribution, and PHI isolation all rest on RBAC discipline alone.

High

No semantic layer — metrics are redefined per BI tool

Tableau, Power BI and Excel each carry their own calculated fields and business logic.

Why it matters — the same KPI returns different numbers in different tools, which is usually how the "we need one source of truth" conversation starts.

High

Workspace has no governed lifecycleconfirm

The analyst sandbox appears to serve reporting directly, with no confirmed retention, PHI restriction, or promotion path into Gold.

Why it matters — an ungoverned sandbox becomes the de-facto source of truth, and the one place PHI controls were never applied.

High

Excel as a first-class consumption tier

Data leaves the governed perimeter as extracts, carrying business logic with it.

Why it matters — extracts cannot be masked, versioned, audited or revoked once distributed — a live PHI exposure path.

High

No disaster-recovery posture

Single account, single region, no replication or failover group in evidence.

Why it matters — no defined RTO/RPO for a platform serving clinical and operational reporting.

Medium

No catalog or lineage for discovery

No evidence of a catalog; dbt lineage may exist but is not established as the discovery surface for analysts.

Why it matters — analysts who cannot find a trusted model rebuild it — which re-creates the duplication problem continuously.

Medium

No data quality gates between layers

No confirmed test suite or promotion criteria governing Silver → Gold.

Why it matters — without gates, "curated" is a naming convention rather than a guarantee.

Medium

The Common layer has no stated purposeconfirm

It sits alongside the medallion layers but its contract is undefined.

Why it matters — undefined shared areas accumulate orphaned objects and become a second, competing home for shared dimensions.

Medium

S3 is barely used

Landing-zone usage is minimal, with no archival, cold-storage or Iceberg strategy.

Why it matters — a missed lever for storage cost and for retaining raw history cheaply outside Snowflake.

Section 3 · Target state

The redesign — three insertions, not a rebuild

Corewell's tooling stays exactly as it is: Astronomer, Fivetran, dbt Core, Snowflake, and the existing BI estate. The redesign inserts two missing layers and one cross-cutting overlay — the pieces that make a single source of truth structurally enforceable rather than a matter of convention.

Structural move 1

Define core entities once

Stand up a conformed layer between Silver and Gold holding Patient, Provider, Encounter, Facility and Payer. Central ownership, versioned in dbt, consumed by every domain mart. This is the mechanism that makes "single source of truth" enforceable rather than aspirational.

Structural move 2

Put metrics above Gold, not inside BI

Move business logic out of Tableau calculated fields and Power BI measures into a shared semantic layer. The test: the same KPI queried from any tool returns the same number, and changing its definition is a single edit.

Structural move 3

Attach protection to tags, not tables

Classify PHI/PII at ingest, tag the columns, and bind masking and row access policies to those tags at Silver. Protection then travels with the data into Gold, Share and Workspace instead of being reapplied — and missed — per object.

Supporting

Close the bypass routes

Give Workspace a real lifecycle — masked data only, TTL on objects, and a promotion path into Gold. Make Share the only external exposure point. These remove the paths by which ungoverned numbers currently reach a dashboard.

Section 4 · Sequencing

How to get there without stopping delivery

Each phase produces something demonstrable. The first two require no platform change — they are discovery and a contained pilot, which also generate the evidence the assessment workbook needs.

Weeks 1–4

Establish the facts

  • Inventory every definition of the core entities across domains
  • Catalogue where the same KPI disagrees between tools
  • PHI/PII column inventory and draft tag taxonomy
  • Close out Jay's tech-debt docs and Angela/Kevin's access
Weeks 5–8

Prove it on one entity

  • Build the conformed Patient entity end to end
  • Repoint one existing Gold mart to consume it
  • Pilot tag-based masking and one row access policy
  • RBAC role hierarchy redesign, applied in sandbox
Weeks 9–12

Make it the default

  • Semantic layer pilot across Tableau and Power BI
  • Workspace governance: TTL, masked-only, promotion path
  • Silver → Gold quality gates in dbt
  • Publish layer contracts as written standards
Quarter 2+

Scale and harden

  • Roll conformed entities across remaining domains
  • Separate Prod from Non-Prod accounts
  • Catalog and lineage as the analyst discovery surface
  • DR: replication, failover group, first drill

Section 5 · Before this is presented

Open questions to confirm with Corewell

Each of these was inferred from the architecture diagram rather than stated in the call. Confirming them either removes a gap from the list or sharpens it.

Q1
What is the Common layer for? If it already holds shared dimensions, the conformed-core work may extend it rather than introduce a new layer.
Q2
Do Tableau, Power BI or Excel read from Workspace or Silver today, or only from Gold/Share? This determines whether the bypass gap is real or theoretical.
Q3
Are any masking or row access policies already deployed? The call framed them as a requirement — confirm nothing exists before reporting it as absent.
Q4
Is Tableau in the estate? The call named Excel and Power BI; the diagram shows Tableau. It changes the BI-rationalization scope.
Q5
What dbt tests and CI run today? Determines whether quality gates are a new build or an extension of existing practice.
Q6
Is there a defined RTO/RPO, or any replication configured? Sizes the DR gap.
