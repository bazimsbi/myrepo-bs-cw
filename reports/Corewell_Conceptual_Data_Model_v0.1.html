Executive summary

This draft conforms two of the three data sources named in discovery — MHA hospital discharge data and Trilliant Health's market intelligence feed. Epic and Strata are not yet represented and their absence is significant: both external sources are de-identified or tokenized, so neither can anchor an enterprise model on its own.

Eleven canonical entities are identified. Provider (via NPI) and Diagnosis (via ICD-10-CM) conform cleanly across both sources today. Person, Visit, and Service Line do not — no shared person key exists between the sources, visit grain differs, and service line is defined in Trilliant but must be derived for MHA.

Section 5 lists the specific inputs — data, decisions, and confirmations — required from Corewell to move this from a two-source sketch to an enterprise model.

Section 3 · Grain Reconciliation

Four sources, four grains, one proposed target

Trilliant reports at procedure line, MHA at discharge, and Epic/Strata — not yet reviewed — are expected at encounter and cost-case grain respectively. The recommended target is visit/encounter, with procedure-line detail kept as a separate linked fact rather than forced up to the coarser grain.

Section 4 · Structural Constraints

Four findings that shape every downstream decision

These aren't modeling preferences — they're hard limits in the source data itself, found while building the matrix above.

Hard limit

Person-level joins between MHA and Trilliant are not possible

MHA carries no patient identifier of any kind — not even a token. Trilliant's person_id is Trilliant's own token, unrelated to any Corewell or MHA identifier. There is no key, direct or indirect, linking the same human across sources.

Consequence — patient-level analysis must stay within a single source. Cross-source work is limited to NPI, geography, date, code sets, and payer.

Hard limit

Geography granularity conflicts — ZIP5 vs. ZIP3

MHA reports patient ZIPCODE at 5 digits. Trilliant's person geography (visit_person_zip3, person_service_area_zip_code) is 3-digit.

Consequence — a conformed geography dimension must degrade MHA to ZIP3, or the two sources maintain separate geography dimensions. This is a business call — see Q8 in Section 5 — not something to decide unilaterally.

Needs decision

Service line is defined in one source and must be derived in the other

Trilliant provides service_line directly, alongside explicit visit_has_surgical / _oncology / _behavioral flags. MHA has no such field — it would be derived from MSDRG/MSMDC (inpatient) or procedure code categories (outpatient).

Consequence — since this is a Strategy & Market Intelligence engagement, service line is arguably the single most important conformed dimension — and the two derivations will disagree unless one canonical taxonomy is built and both sources are mapped into it.

Needs decision

MHA and Trilliant substantially overlap — this is a conflict, not redundancy

Both sources carry visits, diagnoses, procedures, providers and payers. MHA is Michigan hospital discharge data; Trilliant is broader (national, ambulatory/professional, person-longitudinal, with charges).

Consequence — when the two disagree on the same metric for the same period, one must be ruled authoritative. That is a business decision — see Q5.

Section 5 · Completing the Model

What's needed from Corewell

Grouped by type. Sourcing items unblock the technical model; business and governance items determine what "correct" even means for it.

Sourcing — unblocks the technical model

  • Q1Can we get the Epic (Clarity or Caboodle) and Strata data dictionaries — the two sources named in discovery that we still don't have?
  • Q2Which HOSPITALID(s) in the MHA file are Corewell's facilities? Without this, market-share analysis has no "us vs. market" split.
  • Q3Which Trilliant datasets are actually licensed beyond Visits and Organization Directory — Provider Directory, Demand Forecast, Price Transparency, Site Selection?

Business direction — determines correct scope

  • Q4What are the top 5–10 reports or questions the Strategy & Market Intelligence team needs answered first?
  • Q5When MHA and Trilliant disagree on the same metric, which source is authoritative?
  • Q6Is a service line taxonomy already defined anywhere internally, or does it need to be built from scratch?

Governance — constrains how the model can be built

  • Q7What are the MHA data-use-agreement restrictions on redistribution and access within Snowflake?
  • Q8Is ZIP3 an acceptable geography grain for market analysis, or does the business require ZIP5 — meaning MHA cannot be safely conformed down?
